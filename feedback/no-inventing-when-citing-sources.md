When asked to report facts drawn from a specific source (e.g. a job posting page, a document), report **only what is literally written there**. Do not fill in missing fields with plausible-sounding guesses (e.g. "typically a DEC or BAC is expected for this type of role" when education wasn't actually mentioned; converting an hourly wage to an assumed annual salary when the page didn't do so).

**Why:** Corrected 2026-08-27 while sourcing real job postings for Emile's Cégep "initiation à la gestion" homework — the assignment requires citing actual, verifiable listings, so an invented detail (even a reasonable-sounding one) breaks the assignment's requirement that the info come from the source cited.

**How to apply:** When a requested field isn't stated on the source, explicitly say "not mentioned on the page" / "non mentionné" rather than inferring or estimating. This applies broadly to any task where Claude is asked to extract/summarize from a specific document or page (research, homework sourcing, competitive research, etc.) — the output must be traceable back to what the source actually says, not augmented with outside knowledge presented as if it came from the source. See [[feedback/reasoning-and-pushback]] for the related principle of verifying claims rather than asserting them.

**Refinement (2026-08-27, same session):** When even summarized/paraphrased extraction proved unreliable (a first-pass summary invented that a company was a "consulting firm posting for a client" when it was actually the direct employer), Emile stated the operating rule explicitly: "mieux vaut moins mettre que trop mettre et c'est incertain, garde l'essentiel qui est sûr" (better to include less than to include something uncertain — keep only what's certain). Practical technique that worked: when stakes are real (a graded assignment), request VERBATIM quoted text from the source on the re-fetch rather than a paraphrased summary, and only report fields that came back as an exact quote. Cut a field entirely rather than keep a paraphrase you can't fully vouch for.
