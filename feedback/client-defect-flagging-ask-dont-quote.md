When a draft to a client raises an issue Emile noticed but the client didn't ask about (e.g. missing polymeric sand across a whole yard, spotted from photos), don't propose a fix or bundle it into the price of the thing the client did ask about — especially when the issue is broader than the specific spot being quoted.

**Why:** Emile corrected this on the Stéphane Pelletier draft (2026-08-17) — I had scoped a "let's add sand while the stones are up" offer to just the sunken patio section he asked about, but the missing-sand problem is yard-wide, not just that spot. He can't guarantee the client will pay for that separately, so he didn't want it to read as an upsell riding on the unrelated quote.

**How to apply:** raise the observation as a direct, low-pressure question instead (e.g. "when was this last done, was polymeric sand installed?"), state briefly why it matters (weeds, will get worse), and say it can be discussed later/separately — don't fold it into the price or timeline of the thing actually being quoted.
